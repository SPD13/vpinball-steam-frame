# Designing a lobby room

How the two lobby rooms (the modern room and the game room) were designed and built, written so that another session can design the
next one without rediscovering anything. The commands are in `README.md`, the licenses in `CREDITS.md`; this document is the process,
the decisions behind it, and the traps.

## 1. What a lobby room is

- A VPX table holding **only a room**: one primitive per *bake group* (floor, walls, ceiling, furniture...), each with one texture in
  which the lighting is already baked. The primitives are unlit (`disable_lighting_top = 1`), static, without back faces, with a white
  material: VPX shows the texture as it is, so a room costs a handful of draw calls and no lighting per frame on the Frame.
- The primitives sit in the part group `LobbyRoom` with the **room space reference**: in VR, z = 0 is the real floor. The player stands at
  the middle of the bottom edge of the table (`(left + right) / 2, bottom`), and the VR menu panel stands in front of them.
- The launcher loads it instead of a table (`Launcher::GetLobbyTablePath()`), and `BuildLobby()` keeps its parts (`IsLobbyRoomFile`).
  The player picks it in **VR & Room › Choose a lobby room** (`LobbyRoomsPage`, tiles with an image of each room); the choice is
  `Standalone.LobbyRoom`, saved at once. The game room is the default.
- Everything is generated by scripts (Blender headless, numpy, vpxtool): nothing is edited by hand, so a room can be rebuilt, tweaked and
  rebaked by running the scripts again.

## 2. The process, step by step

### 2.1 Brief

Start from a style or a reference picture, and write down its ingredients, not its content: for the game room the reference gave "night,
floor-to-ceiling window on a city, warm LED cove on one wall, black track spots, arcade cabinets, a pinball, cognac leather seats, orange
stools, wood coffee table, patterned rug, big plant, neon sign". Nothing of the picture itself is reused (no logo, no game, no artwork).

Then place things for VR, in Blender meters, the player at the origin facing **+Y**:

- The **menu panel** appears about 1.2 to 1.5 m in front of the player, around eye height. Keep a clear corridor in front of them
  (roughly |x| < 1.2 m, y from 0 to 3 m) and give the panel a calm, not too bright background (the slat wall, the window).
- **Size**: 8 to 9 m wide, 8.5 m deep, 3 to 3.2 m high, the player 3 m from the back wall. Bigger rooms feel empty; nearer walls feel
  cramped in the headset.
- Furniture along the walls and in the corners, a door on the back wall (the player rarely looks back, but it must not be blank).
- Budget: about 100k triangles and five to eight textures in all (see 2.5).

### 2.2 Assets: licenses first

Only **CC0** (or CC-BY, credited in `CREDITS.md`) assets, or things made by the scripts. The fork ships the files in an open repository,
so the license must allow redistributing the raw files.

- **Poly Haven** (all CC0): textures, models (glTF), HDRIs. `tools/fetch_polyhaven.py downloads texture:<id> model:<id> hdri:<id>:8k`
  downloads them through its API and records name, authors and license in `downloads/credits.json`, from which `CREDITS.md` is written.
  The catalog can be listed with `https://api.polyhaven.com/assets?type=models|textures|hdris` (filter on `categories`), previews are at
  `https://cdn.polyhaven.com/asset_img/primary/<id>.png?width=800`. Some textures have no diffuse map but color variants (`coll1`):
  handled by the fetch tool. `tools/inspect_props.py downloads` prints the size and triangle count of each model: check before
  using one (some "plants" are millions of triangles, some sofas are 2.7 m long, some models hold several variants side by side, see
  `import_prop(pick=)`).
- Other CC0 sources worth a look: ambientCG (textures), Kenney and Quaternius (stylized low-poly kits).
- **Never**: NC or ND licenses, Sketchfab "Standard/Editorial", store assets (Unity, Unreal, marketplaces), models ripped from games,
  VPX community VR rooms (no license, reused models).
- **Made by the scripts** when nothing fits: the arcade cabinets, the pinball, the sofa, stools, track lights, neon sign of the game room
  are boxes, prisms and cylinders (`roomkit.py`). Their artwork is drawn with numpy (`gameroom_art.py`): pixel art screens with sprites
  of our own, marquees with invented titles in a 5x7 pixel font, playfield and backglass. Keep away from look-alikes of famous games
  (the first aliens looked too much like a well-known shooter and were redrawn) and from symbols with a meaning (a star made of two
  triangles was replaced by an arrow). Text uses `src/assets/LiberationSans-Regular.ttf` (OFL, shipped with VPX).

### 2.3 Scene script

One script per room (`build_room.py`, `build_gameroom.py`), using the helpers of `roomkit.py`:

- `pbr_material(name, folder, scale, tint, roughness_mul)` for Poly Haven textures tiled in meters, `flat_material`,
  `emissive_material`, `image_material(name, rgb_array, emission=)` for generated images (screens, art).
- `quad`, `box(skip=...)` (drop the faces never seen), `prism(profile)` (a side profile extruded: cabinets, pinball body),
  `cylinder`, `face_toward(bm, point)`, `box_uv` (UVs in meters), `transform(objs, matrix)` to build a piece at the origin and move it.
- `import_prop(asset, location, rot_z_deg, scale, pick, tint)`. Poly Haven models face **+Y**: rotate by -90° to face +X, +90° to face -X.
- Lights: `spot`, `area`, `point`; emissive meshes for LED strips, screens and neon also light the room in the bake.
- Every mesh gets `obj["bake_group"]`; the scene lists `scene["bake_sizes"]` (texture size per group), `scene["bake_emit_only"]`
  (groups with nothing to light, like a backdrop) and `scene["bake_hide_when_lighting"]` (heavy emitters hidden while the others are
  lit, see 2.5).

Iterate with previews before any bake: `render_views.py <blend> -- <dir> 32 front left right back corner` renders from the eye
(1.65 m) and from a corner in about 30 s per view at 32 samples; look at them, fix, rebuild. The previews use AgX like VPX, so the
mood carries over; glossy reflections do not (the bake is view independent), so judge the floor's shine in VPX, not in the previews.

### 2.4 A view outside (the windows of the game room and the studio)

The city is a Poly Haven HDRI. It lights the room as the world (the 4k file) and is seen through the window on a **backdrop**: a
cylinder 70 m away whose emission looks the panorama up along the direction from the player's eye, baked once (emit only, the 8k file).
From 70 m the parallax error of a moving head is negligible. The rotation of the panorama (`HDRI_ROTATION`) was found by rendering
the window view at three angles. Ground-level panoramas show the street right below the horizon: the backdrop looks the panorama
up 5° higher than the real direction (`HORIZON_LIFT`), which sinks the crowd along the river below a 1.15 m parapet and makes the view
read as a high floor. A terrace floor and the parapet (bake group `exterior`) fill the space between the window and the backdrop.

Both are `roomkit.world_environment` and `roomkit.backdrop(facing_deg=...)`. The backdrop is **seen by the camera only** (its ray
visibility is off for diffuse, glossy and shadow rays): the light of the room comes from the world, where Cycles samples the sun of
the panorama as a light. On a mesh the sun would only be found by chance, and the sunlit studio came out dark.

**Sunlight** (the studio): find the sun of the panorama (its brightest pixel) and turn the panorama so that the sun stands outside the
windows. Blender's equirectangular mapping is `u = 0.5 − atan2(y, x) / 2π`, `v = 0.5 + elevation / π` (rows of `image.pixels` from
the bottom), so the sun's azimuth in the image is `a = (0.5 − u) · 2π`, and the Mapping node (Point, rotation θ around Z) gives it the
azimuth `a − θ` in the room: θ = a − the azimuth wanted. Getting the sign of `u` wrong put the sun outside the wall without windows.
A low sun (26° in `garden_nook`) throws long sunbeams across the floor with the shadows of the window crosses, as in a photo.

### 2.5 Bake

`bake.py <blend> -- <out dir> <samples>` joins the objects of each group, unwraps a `Lightmap` UV layout (smart project + pack,
12 texel margin) and bakes with Cycles on the M1 GPU and CPU: diffuse color x diffuse light, plus glossy color x glossy light, plus
emission. The light passes are denoised with OpenImageDenoise (through a compositor scene that renders nothing), so the color passes
need only 4 samples and the textures stay sharp. Output: one linear EXR per group and `baked.blend` (the joined meshes and their UVs).

- **Sizes**: about 400 texels per meter on floors and walls, more on furniture seen up close: 4096 for floor, walls, furniture,
  cabinets, the backdrop; 2048 for ceiling, small groups. VPX compresses them to BC7 at first load (16 MB per 4096 texture).
- **Samples**: 32 for a test (10 to 15 min), 128 for the final (44 min for the modern room, 47 min for the game room).
- **Memory**: the Mac has 16 GB and the colima VM of the Frame build takes 8 GB. An 8k HDRI loaded twice (world and backdrop) made one
  group swap for an hour; hence the 4k world and `bake_hide_when_lighting`. Do not run a Frame build during a bake.
- Read the luminance percentiles printed per group: the surfaces of both rooms stay below 1 (p99 0.5 to 0.9), which sets the export
  scale (2.6).

### 2.6 Export to VPX

`export_vpx.py <bake dir>/baked.blend -- <bake dir> <base dir> <out.vpx> 1 "<Room name>"`, where the base dir is `vpxtool extract`
of `src/assets/blankTable.vpx` (its settings are kept, its parts and images replaced):

- **Axes**: Blender (x, y, z) meters → VPX (center + x, bottom − y, z) VP units (`MTOVPU`, 1852.6 per meter), written as OBJ the way
  VPX exports (Z negated, which is a rotation of the Blender axes and keeps the winding).
- **Tone**: textures hold linear values / `scale` (1) as sRGB 8 bits, the table uses AgX and exposure `scale`, matching the previews.
  Lights brighter than 1 are scaled down keeping their hue (a pink neon stays pink).
- **Near plane marker**: outside VR, VPX derives the near clipping plane from the bounding boxes of the parts, so a few large parts
  would clip the floor in front of the player; a 2 cm quad hidden 1 cm under the floor, 0.5 m ahead, keeps it close.
- Then `cp <out.vpx> src/assets/`. `*.vpx` is ignored by `.git/info/exclude` in the main checkout: `git add -f`.

### 2.7 Check in VPX, make the thumbnail

On the Mac (`vpinball/build`, see the CLAUDE.md of the workspace for the build), with an isolated preferences folder holding a copy of
the user's `VPinballX.ini` and `LobbyRoom = <value>` in `[Standalone]`:

```sh
python3 steamframe/lobby-room/tools/lobby-shot-hooks.py apply     # temporary hook, rebuild the app after it
cp src/assets/<room>.vpx build/VPinballX_BGFX.app/Contents/Resources/assets/
VPX_TEST_NOUI=1 VPX_TEST_SHOT=/tmp/room.png build/VPinballX_BGFX.app/Contents/MacOS/VPinballX_BGFX -PrefPath <prefs> -Launcher &
# saves the lobby without the menu after 4.5 s and quits; VPX_TEST_PAGE=lobby/rooms shows the room chooser instead
python3 steamframe/lobby-room/tools/lobby-shot-hooks.py remove    # before committing, rebuild again
```

Stop VPX by its process id only (other sessions run their own instances). The desktop view is a level camera at the height of the
playfield (`BuildLobby`, `mViewZ = 0`, FOV 60°). The thumbnail is that screenshot cropped away from the pause icon and scaled:
`ffmpeg -i room.png -vf "crop=1360:765:40:45,scale=640:360" -q:v 3 src/assets/lobby-rooms/<name>.jpg`.

### 2.8 Register the room

- `LOBBY_ROOMS` in `src/steamframe/Launcher.cpp`: `{ value, name, table file, thumbnail }`, in the order of the chooser.
- `Standalone.LobbyRoom` in `src/core/Settings_properties.inl` (the `VPX_STEAM_FRAME` block): append the name to the enum (values are
  saved in users' settings: never renumber, only append).
- `CREDITS.md` (every asset, with authors and license), `README.md` (the table of rooms), `docs/Steam Frame Branch.md` (lobby rooms).
- Commit as fixups of their area commits (`steamframe/README.md`, "Syncing with upstream"): the setting to "core hooks", the launcher
  code, tables and thumbnails to "launcher, table library, table picker, scores", the scripts and documents to "README, documentation
  and sync tools", the VR menu to "VR UI". Pushes to `fork/master` are fast-forwards; never force-push without the owner.

### 2.9 Test on the Frame

`Doc/steam-frame-build/build-linux-arm64.sh all` (about 10 min) then `Doc/steam-frame-build/deploy-to-frame.sh`; when only assets
changed, copying them into `steam-frame-package/assets/` and deploying is enough. The owner checks every room in the headset: where it
sits around the player, its scale (ceiling, distance of the walls), the view through windows in stereo, the menu over its background.

## 3. Traps already met

- bmesh faces built by hand have no normal until `bm.normal_update()`: UVs projected from them collapse (the floor showed streaks).
- Faces of the shell must face the inside of the room (`face_toward`): Blender renders both sides, VPX culls back faces.
- Images loaded with relative paths turn pink once the .blend is saved elsewhere: load them with absolute paths.
- `o.bound_box` is stale after `o.data.transform`: compute extents from the vertices.
- Blender 5 compositor: `scene.compositing_node_group` with a `NodeGroupOutput`, not the old `scene.node_tree`.
- zsh does not split `$var` into words (`set -- $cfg` takes the whole string).
- The Mac build may need `platforms/macos-arm64/external.sh` after an upstream sync adds a dependency.
- The `cargo` in `/usr/local` is x86 and cannot run: vpxtool comes from its macOS aarch64 release (`~/.local/bin/vpxtool`).
- The thumbnails of rooms are screenshots of the real lobby: retake them when a room changes.
- Wall objects of Poly Haven (the dartboard) face −Y with their back at the origin, unlike the furniture, which faces +Y: check
  the facing of each model in a preview (the dartboard of the game room first faced its wall). Some models are built sideways (the
  display shelves).

## 4. Ideas for next rooms

Any style works as long as its ingredients can come from CC0 assets or simple procedural shapes: a retro arcade hall (more cabinets,
carpet with a pattern drawn in numpy), a garage workshop (Poly Haven has tool carts, shelves, an old bed frame...), a beach bar at sunset
(a sunset HDRI on the backdrop), a space station (procedural panels, a planet backdrop drawn in numpy). Keep the clear corridor in front
of the player and a calm background for the menu.
